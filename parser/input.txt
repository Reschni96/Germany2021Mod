CDU Course-Correction wenn Mitte
EQ9: Up until now, you have seemingly defused your rhetoric a bit, embracing a classical moderate-conservative Christian Democracy, mostly following the center-right party doctrine. Some advisors want you to pivot further left towards the median voter, while others want you to take the AfD’s wind out of their sails. In an internal meeting, you’re asked frankly: where are taking us, Fritz?
A1: I want to take this course-correction as a sign for unity and reconciliation. I want to make up with the Merkel-wing of the Union and the party elites as a whole. I am one of them, and I plan to run as a mainstream Union politician. We should invest into forging new connections and making new friends inside the system.
F1: Your earnest efforts to reconcile bear fruit. Even Merkel seems to warm up to you, as the party elites of both CDU and CSU are happy to be coddled. To those who elected you as a right-wing outsider, this is a betrayal, but you know as much as anyone that connections are everything. Time to continue working with your colleagues - the centrist age will continue under your guidance.
A2: The CDU is the party of the center. We have done a great job at governing over the last 16 years, and while we need to move into the future, our future is a Christian-Democratic one. I stand by my course.
F2: Steady she goes. Your framing satisfies everyone in the room and isn’t out of character for you. Nodding to each side of the aisle keeps your party united and conflict-free - a nice contrast to your past combative behavior. You see a small rise in your favorability, even if you seem boring to others.
A3: I have been voted in by a particular base - Merkel-skeptic conservatives. The chancellor has become popular with voters of the other parties - for us, some of her policies are a bit more controversial. On these issues, we need to move a bit to the right - to provide for a real alternative to Greens and SPD.
F3: Steady she goes. This is an expected tactic on your behalf. The word “alternative” isn’t lost to your advisors, and the return to your conservative bonafides solidifies your right flank. You have less competition on this side of the spectrum, so you might come out in first place by default - but there are the usual internal grumblings about your warring with Merkel.
A4: Let’s shake this up a bit. I am standing for what’s right, and what gets me elected. We need to rhetorically escalate on cultural issues and go with what’s popular economically - in this age, you can’t rely on the dogmata of old. Ideology be damned - it’s time for a new course, it’s time to listen to Germany First.
F4: And just like that, you’ve once again pissed off the party elites. But then again - maybe this madness is included in your calculations? You seem to understand something many don’t: in the postfactual age, this populist rabble-rousing seems to resonate with a previously invisible voter base… time to triangulate, time to get out the pitchforks.
